\section{The problem}

With the code carried between blocks (\code{code\_residual}) hard Top-\(K'\) gains \(0.06\)--\(0.075\) nats at every
\(K'\), while RBLapSum (\(K\) active, pool \(K{+}J\)) gains \(-0.03\) to \(+0.015\): at a matched active count it is
worth \(0.047\) at \(K=32\) and loses \(0.02\)--\(0.03\) at \(K=128, 256\), no cell reaches hard Top-\((K{+}J)\), and its
backward is the unstable part of the grid (\code{docs/kj-vs-hard-topk-code-residual.tex}).  The first-order scope
removes the instability with a second backward pass and is \emph{worse} than the unmodified surrogate at every
\(K=32\) cell (\(+0.007\) to \(+0.017\) at 20k).  This note locates the conflict between the surrogate and the carry
and proposes the smallest change that removes it, at no cost.

\section{Where the probabilities act}

Gate \(\ell\) reads \(u_\ell=c_\ell+v_\ell\) (carried code plus the block's encoded contribution) and outputs
\(c_{\ell+1}=M_\ell u_\ell\), \(M_\ell\) the Top-\(K\) mask.  Its output has two consumers, the carry
\(u_{\ell+1}=c_{\ell+1}+v_{\ell+1}\) (a skip path) and the read \(x_{\ell+1}=\mathrm N(D_\ell c_{\ell+1})\) (a branch), so
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial u_\ell}=M_\ell\bigl(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}+r_{\ell+1}\bigr),\qquad
  g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial u_{\ell+1}},\quad
  r_{\ell+1}=D_\ell^{\!\top}\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(D_\ell c_{\ell+1})}
  \label{eq:hard}
\end{equation}
(at the last gate \(r\) is the unembedding's gradient).  In the stream-carried stack the gate has only the second
consumer: everything block \(\ell{+}1\) does, its own gate included, goes through \(D_\ell\).  RBLapSum relaxes the
gate as a whole, \(M_\ell\to M_\ell+S_\ell\) with the \code{through\_rank\_kappa} support term \(S_\ell\) (kernel
\(\kappa_i=e^{-|s_i-b|/T}/2T\); on the support \(S_{ii}=|u_i|\kappa_i\approx b/2T\) at the boundary).  In the stream
stack \(S_\ell\) acts on \(r_{\ell+1}\) alone, and what reaches gate \(\ell{-}1\) has passed through \(E_\ell\), the
block and \(D_{\ell-1}\), which mix the coordinates.  In the code stack it also acts on \(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}\), which
already contains \(S_{\ell+1}g^{\rm c}_{\ell+2}\): along a coordinate near the boundary the backward multiplies
\(1+|u|\kappa\) per gate.  The carry is a residual connection in code space; a residual connection trains because its
backward is the identity, and a relaxation placed on the skip path compounds.  The conflict is \emph{where} the
probabilities act, not the kernel: the same gates in the stream stack were never on a skip path.

\section{The carry-aware surrogate}

Keep the skip path hard and relax the branch.  The gate's output is split into the copy the next gate carries and the
copy the decoder reads (\code{\_CarryReadSplit}; forward and inference unchanged), and the backward is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial u_\ell}=M_\ell\bigl(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}+r_{\ell+1}\bigr)+S_\ell^{\!\top}\hat g_{\ell+1},
  \qquad
  \hat g_{\ell+1,i}=r_{\ell+1,i}+\begin{cases}0 & \text{local}\\ H_{\ell+1,i} & \text{hard}\\ \rho R_{\ell+1,i} & \text{persistent,}\end{cases}
  \label{eq:carry}
\end{equation}
with \(H_\ell=M_\ell(r_{\ell+1}+H_{\ell+1})\) the downstream read gradients a coordinate reaches along the hard carry,
\(R_\ell=r_{\ell+1}+\rho R_{\ell+1}\) all downstream reads weighted by a survival factor \(\rho\) per gate, and
\(H=R=0\) above the last gate.  The support term is driven by read gradients only, so no kernel sees another gate's
support term through the carry: the exponential products are gone, the terms of the gates above still reach gate
\(\ell\) added through \(M_\ell\), and carry and update receive one gradient (R1 of
\code{docs/rblapsum-code-residual.tex}).  The cost is two per-coordinate sums along the backward, no second pass.
The drivers are the first-order loss change of one flip at gate \(\ell\) under three models of what follows: a flip
changes \(c_{\ell+1,i}\) by \(\pm u_{\ell,i}\), which block \(\ell{+}1\) reads, and so do later blocks while the coordinate
stays in the code.  \emph{Local} counts the next read only -- the stream stack's logic, and exactly the gradient of a
model whose decoder reads a relaxed gate while the carry is hard.  \emph{Hard} follows the coordinate's actual
downstream masks (an evicted feature's remaining run; for an inactive candidate almost always nothing).
\emph{Persistent} assumes the entering coordinate survives each further gate with probability \(\rho\) (\(\rho=1\):
to the end).  The \emph{mixed} scope uses hard for the active members and persistent for the inactive ones, the
natural model on each side; \(\rho\) is measured in Section~\ref{sec:runs}.  Against the first-order estimator: that
one needs the fully hard gradient at every gate, hence a second pass; here the carry is where the gradient is taken
hard, and the reads still carry the gates above through the branch, as in the stream stack.

\section{Measurements}

\textbf{Setup.}  Everything is the code-carried \(T=2\) arm of the K+J grid: 8\(\times\)768, \(N=1536\),
\code{residual\_out} at every block, no output norm, batch \(96\times512\), the 20k-step cosine schedule, seed 1337.
The baselines are the grid's own runs (\code{runs\_madrid}: hard Top-\(K'\) with the post-norm, RBLapSum \code{pool},
and the \code{first\_order} sweep cells).  Each new scope is trained with \code{train\_guard.py --stop-step 3000} on
the grid's config with one field changed, so a step-matched comparison with the 20k runs is exact up to the GPU
(one RTX 5090 here, eight on madrid; same generation).  Three thousand steps is where the grid's surrogate margins
are largest and already of the sign they keep: at \(K=32\) the \code{pool} cell is \(-0.061\) below hard Top-32 at
step 3000 and \(-0.042\) at 20k, at \(K=256\) it is \(+0.048\) above hard Top-256 at 3000 and \(+0.034\) at 20k.

\textbf{The backward at initialization} (Fig.~\ref{fig:init}).  With the weights at initialization and one validation
batch, the RMS gradient reaching the carried code under the \code{pool} scope grows by \(\times1.5\) per gate toward
the input at \(K=32\) and \(\times1.4\) at \(K=256\): \(22\times\) and \(12\times\) the hard backward at gate 0
(\(46\times\) at \(T=1\)).  Under every carry scope the profile is a sum, not a product: \code{carry\_local} stays at
\(1.7\)--\(2.1\times\) at every gate, \code{carry\_hard} reaches \(6.2\times\), \code{carry\_mixed} \(7.2\times\) and
\code{carry\_persistent} \(8.4\times\) at gate 0 (\(K=32\)), the first-order estimator \(4.4\times\) -- each gate's
term added once and carried down through the masks.  The surrogate's share of the gradient energy at the gates,
\(\sigma=\lVert S^\top\hat g\rVert^2/\lVert m\odot g\rVert^2\) averaged over gates, is \(0.70\) for \code{pool},
\(0.74\) for \code{carry\_mixed}, \(0.31\) for \code{carry\_local} and \(0.24\) for first order at \(K=32\)
(\(0.30\), \(0.20\), \(0.09\), \(0.09\) at \(K=256\)): the mixed scope injects as much support signal as the unmodified
backward, additively.


\textbf{Runs of the carried code} (Fig.~\ref{fig:runs}; \code{analysis/code\_runs.py}, 16 validation sequences, hard
forward).\label{sec:runs}  The carry is persistent for a subset of the code, not for the code.  In the trained hard
Top-32 model every gate from block 1 on evicts \(30\)--\(54\%\) of the support it receives and fills the slots with
new entries; a feature that enters is still in the support one gate later with probability \(0.51\), two gates later
\(0.34\), and \(12\%\) of the final code was carried from gate 0 while \(54\%\) entered at the last gate (hard
Top-256: \(0.57\), \(0.41\), \(17\%\), \(34\%\)).  Summing the survival curve, an entry at gate 0 is read by
\(2.7\) (\(K{=}32\)) to \(3.1\) (\(K{=}256\)) blocks on average, against 8 under the persistent model and 1 under the
local one.  The \code{pool} surrogate trains the model \emph{out of} the carry: at \((32,224)\) its turnover is
\(55\%\) per gate at step 2000 and \(70\%\) at 20k, survival one gate later \(0.27\to0.16\), and \(77\%\) of its final
code enters at the last gate -- the re-selection at every block of the stream-carried stack, reproduced by a model
that has a carry.  This is why the carry was ``nearly neutral'' for the surrogate in the grid: the surrogate-trained
model hardly uses it.

\section{What the measurements show}

\textbf{The conflict is the placement, not the kernel.}  RBLapSum relaxes a gate whose output, in the code-carried
stack, is also a skip connection; the relaxation is multiplied along the skip path and grows as \((1+b/2T)^{L}\)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:init}).  Putting the probabilities where they were in the stream-carried stack, on what the decoder
reads, and leaving the carry's backward the mask, removes the product exactly, in one backward pass, and gives the
carry and the update one gradient.  \PH{training evidence}

\textbf{The carry is used by a subset, and the unmodified surrogate trains it away.}  \PH{runs of the carry scopes}

\PH{large K}

\textbf{Scope.}  One seed, one configuration family (8 layers, \(N=1536\), \(T=2\), no output norm), screens of
3000 steps with step-matched 20k-step baselines trained on a different machine of the same GPU generation; the
grid's own early-versus-final margins (\(-0.061\to-0.042\) at \(K=32\), \(+0.048\to+0.034\) at \(K=256\) for
\code{pool}) are the only calibration of what a 3000-step difference becomes.  \PH{long runs}
