\section{The problem}

With the code carried between blocks (\code{code\_residual}, \code{docs/kj-vs-hard-topk-code-residual.tex}) hard
Top-\(K'\) gains \(0.06\)--\(0.075\) nats at every \(K'\), while RBLapSum with \(K\) active features and a pool of
\(K+J\) gains \(-0.03\) to \(+0.015\): at a matched active count the surrogate is worth \(0.047\) nats at \(K=32\) and
loses \(0.02\)--\(0.03\) at \(K=128,256\), and no \((K,J)\) cell reaches hard Top-\((K{+}J)\).  Its backward is also the
unstable part of the grid (spikes at \(T=2\), five collapses at \(T=1\)).  The first-order scope removes the instability
with a second backward pass and is \emph{worse} than the unmodified surrogate at every \(K=32\) cell of the grid
(\(+0.007\) to \(+0.017\) at 20k).  This note asks what in RBLapSum conflicts with the carry, and proposes the smallest
change that removes the conflict at no cost.

\section{Where RBLapSum sits in the two stacks}

Gate \(\ell\) reads \(u_\ell=c_\ell+v_\ell\) (carried code plus the block's encoded contribution \(v_\ell=E_\ell\Delta_\ell\))
and outputs \(c_{\ell+1}=M_\ell u_\ell\), \(M_\ell\) the Top-\(K\) mask.  Its output has two consumers:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{u_{\ell+1}=c_{\ell+1}+v_{\ell+1}}_{\text{the carry (skip path)}},\qquad
  \underbrace{x_{\ell+1}=\mathrm{N}(D_\ell\,c_{\ell+1})}_{\text{the read (branch)}},
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial u_\ell}=M_\ell\bigl(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}+r_{\ell+1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:hard}
\end{equation}
with \(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}=\partial\mathcal L/\partial u_{\ell+1}\) the gradient arriving along the carry and
\(r_{\ell+1}=D_\ell^{\!\top}\partial\mathcal L/\partial(D_\ell c_{\ell+1})\) the gradient of block \(\ell{+}1\)'s read
(the unembedding's, at the last gate).  In the stream-carried stack the gate has only the second consumer: everything
block \(\ell{+}1\) does, including its own gate, goes through \(D_\ell\).  RBLapSum relaxes the gate as a whole,
\(M_\ell\to M_\ell+S_\ell\) with the support term \(S_\ell\) of \code{through\_rank\_kappa} (kernel
\(\kappa_i=e^{-|s_i-b|/T}/2T\), on the support \(S_{ii}=|u_i|\kappa_i\approx b/2T\) at the boundary), and this was
harmless in the stream stack: \(S_\ell\) acts on \(r_{\ell+1}\), and whatever of it reaches gate \(\ell{-}1\) has
passed through \(E_\ell\), the block and \(D_{\ell-1}\), which mix the coordinates.  In the code stack the same
relaxation also acts on \(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}\), which already contains \(S_{\ell+1}\) applied to \(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+2}\),
and so on: along a coordinate that stays near the boundary the backward multiplies \((1+|u|\kappa)\) per gate.  The
carry is a residual connection in code space, and a residual connection is trainable because its backward is the
identity; a relaxation placed on the skip path compounds.  This is the whole conflict, and it is a statement about
\emph{where} the probabilities act, not about the kernel: the same gates in the stream stack were never on a skip path.

\section{The carry-aware surrogate}

Keep the skip path hard and relax the branch.  The gate's output is split into the copy the next gate carries and the
copy the decoder reads (\code{\_CarryReadSplit}; the forward is unchanged, inference is the hard model), and the backward is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial u_\ell}=M_\ell\bigl(g^{\rm c}_{\ell+1}+r_{\ell+1}\bigr)+S_\ell^{\!\top}\hat g_{\ell+1},
  \qquad
  \hat g_{\ell+1,i}=r_{\ell+1,i}+\begin{cases}0 & \text{local}\\ H_{\ell+1,i} & \text{hard}\\ R_{\ell+1,i} & \text{persistent,}\end{cases}
  \label{eq:carry}
\end{equation}
where \(H_\ell=M_\ell(r_{\ell+1}+H_{\ell+1})\) is the sum of the downstream read gradients a coordinate reaches along
the hard carry, \(R_\ell=r_{\ell+1}+R_{\ell+1}\) the sum of all of them, and \(H=R=0\) above the last gate.  The support
term is driven by read gradients only, so no kernel ever sees another gate's support term through the carry: the
products that grow exponentially in the unmodified backward do not exist, the support terms of the gates above still
reach gate \(\ell\)'s input, added, through \(M_\ell\), and the carry and the update receive one gradient (requirement
R1 of \code{docs/rblapsum-code-residual.tex}).  The cost is two per-coordinate sums carried along the backward
(\(O(BTN)\) per gate, no second pass).  The three drivers are what the first-order change of the loss under one flip at
gate \(\ell\) is, under three models of what happens downstream.  A flip changes \(c_{\ell+1,i}\) by \(\pm u_{\ell,i}\),
which block \(\ell{+}1\) reads (\(r_{\ell+1,i}\)) and, if the coordinate stays in the code, so do the blocks after it.
``Local'' is the stream stack's logic: the next read only, which is exactly the gradient of a model in which the
decoder reads a relaxed gate and the carry is hard.  ``Hard'' follows the coordinate's actual downstream masks: for an
evicted active feature these are its remaining run; for an inactive candidate they are almost always zero, so entry
counts one read and eviction counts a whole run.  ``Persistent'' assumes the flipped coordinate is carried to the end.
The \emph{mixed} scope uses hard for the active members and persistent for the inactive ones, the two cases where
each is the natural model (Section~\ref{sec:runs} measures how far it holds); \code{carry\_local} and
\code{carry\_mixed} are the two variants trained below, with \code{carry\_hard} and \code{carry\_persistent} as
ablations.  Against the first-order estimator: that one evaluates every gate's term on the fully hard gradient,
which needs a second pass; here the read gradients \(r\) still carry the gates above through the branch, as in the
stream stack, and the carry is where the hard gradient is taken.  Against a reduced support scale \(\gamma\): that
shrinks every term and keeps the products at order \(\gamma^2\).


\section{Measurements}

\textbf{Setup.}  Everything is the code-carried \(T=2\) arm of the K+J grid: 8\(\times\)768, \(N=1536\),
\code{residual\_out} at every block, no output norm, batch \(96\times512\), the 20k-step cosine schedule, seed 1337.
The baselines are the grid's own runs (\code{runs\_madrid}: hard Top-\(K'\) with the post-norm, RBLapSum \code{pool},
and the \code{first\_order} sweep cells).  Each new scope is trained with \code{train\_guard.py --stop-step 3000} on
the grid's config with one field changed, so a step-matched comparison with the 20k runs is exact up to the GPU
(one RTX 5090 here, eight on madrid; same generation).  Three thousand steps is where the grid's surrogate margins
are largest and already of the sign they keep: at \(K=32\) the \code{pool} cell is \(-0.061\) below hard Top-32 at
step 3000 and \(-0.042\) at 20k, at \(K=256\) it is \(+0.048\) above hard Top-256 at 3000 and \(+0.034\) at 20k.

\textbf{The backward at initialization} (Fig.~\ref{fig:init}).  With the weights at initialization and one validation
batch, the RMS gradient reaching the carried code under the \code{pool} scope grows by \(\times1.5\) per gate toward
the input at \(K=32\) and \(\times1.4\) at \(K=256\): \(22\times\) and \(12\times\) the hard backward at gate 0
(\(46\times\) at \(T=1\)).  Under every carry scope the profile is a sum, not a product: \code{carry\_local} stays at
\(1.7\)--\(2.1\times\) at every gate, \code{carry\_hard} reaches \(6.2\times\), \code{carry\_mixed} \(7.2\times\) and
\code{carry\_persistent} \(8.4\times\) at gate 0 (\(K=32\)), the first-order estimator \(4.4\times\) -- each gate's
term added once and carried down through the masks.  The surrogate's share of the gradient energy at the gates,
\(\sigma=\lVert S^\top\hat g\rVert^2/\lVert m\odot g\rVert^2\) averaged over gates, is \(0.70\) for \code{pool},
\(0.74\) for \code{carry\_mixed}, \(0.31\) for \code{carry\_local} and \(0.24\) for first order at \(K=32\)
(\(0.30\), \(0.20\), \(0.09\), \(0.09\) at \(K=256\)): the mixed scope injects as much support signal as the unmodified
backward, additively.


\textbf{Runs of the carried code} (Fig.~\ref{fig:runs}; \code{analysis/code\_runs.py}, 16 validation sequences, hard
forward).\label{sec:runs}  The carry is persistent for a subset of the code, not for the code.  In the trained hard
Top-32 model every gate from block 1 on evicts \(30\)--\(54\%\) of the support it receives and fills the slots with
new entries; a feature that enters is still in the support one gate later with probability \(0.51\), two gates later
\(0.34\), and \(12\%\) of the final code was carried from gate 0 while \(54\%\) entered at the last gate (hard
Top-256: \(0.57\), \(0.41\), \(17\%\), \(34\%\)).  Summing the survival curve, an entry at gate 0 is read by
\(2.7\) (\(K{=}32\)) to \(3.1\) (\(K{=}256\)) blocks on average, against 8 under the persistent model and 1 under the
local one.  The \code{pool} surrogate trains the model \emph{out of} the carry: at \((32,224)\) its turnover is
\(55\%\) per gate at step 2000 and \(70\%\) at 20k, survival one gate later \(0.27\to0.16\), and \(77\%\) of its final
code enters at the last gate -- the re-selection at every block of the stream-carried stack, reproduced by a model
that has a carry.  This is why the carry was ``nearly neutral'' for the surrogate in the grid: the surrogate-trained
model hardly uses it.
