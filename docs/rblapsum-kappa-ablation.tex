% ============================================================
\section{Purpose and summary of conclusions}
% ============================================================

RBLapSum's boundary surrogate can be applied in two forms. In plain
\code{through\_rank}, each candidate's score gradient is the
kernel-weighted boundary term itself. In \code{through\_rank\_kappa}, the
same term is additionally passed through the LapSum rank-one Jacobian at
the boundary,
\[
g = a - q\,{\textstyle\sum_i a_i},\qquad q_i=\kappa_i/{\textstyle\sum_j \kappa_j},
\]
which removes the common score-translation mode. This note tests whether
the kappa correction improves convergence, using matched training runs at a
boundary floor of $b_0=0$ (the new default; the earlier campaigns used
$b_0=0.1$).

Conclusions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The kappa correction is decisively better in every tested
configuration.} Plain \code{through\_rank} diverges within the first
$\sim$1700 steps in all four configurations with $K\geq 64$, and in the two
$K=32$ configurations where it survives it converges $0.085$--$0.164$ nats
worse than \code{through\_rank\_kappa} while showing repeated large
excursions along the way.

\item \textbf{With the correction, five of six configurations converge},
at final validation CE $1.46$--$1.55$; the exception is $(K,J)=(64,64)$ at
$T=1$, which diverged at step 8680. The $K=64$, $T=1$ row is the known
marginal row of this surrogate.

\item \textbf{The boundary floor does not matter in these regimes.} The
four \code{through\_rank\_kappa} configurations that were previously
trained at $b_0=0.1$ reproduce at $b_0=0$ within $\pm0.005$ nats, in line
with the earlier measurement that the mean boundary stays $8$--$40\times$
above the old floor.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================
\section{Setup}
% ============================================================

Twelve runs, all under full magnitude--direction decoupling with the same
architecture, data, optimizer, schedules, and seed as the concurrent
MD-versus-MD-init campaign (8$\times$768, \code{abs\_topk} over $N=1536$,
\code{residual\_out}, batch $96\times512$, 20k steps). The grid pairs the
two gradient modes at
\[
(K,J,T)\in\{(32,32,1),\,(32,480,1),\,(64,64,1),\,(64,448,1),\,
(128,128,2),\,(128,384,2)\},
\]
with $T=2$ for the $K=128$ rows because $K=128$, $T=1$ is known to diverge
under every regime. All runs use $b_0=0$; the run names carry a
\code{b00} marker. Divergence is detected and the run stopped by the same
guard as in the companion note (non-finite CE, or CE far above its own best
for a sustained window).

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

The kappa correction is not an optional refinement of the
\code{through\_rank} surrogate; in these setups it is what makes the
surrogate usable. Without it, training survives only in the most
conservative row tested ($K=32$, $T=1$), and even there at a
$0.085$--$0.164$ nat penalty with visibly bursty dynamics. With it, every
configuration except the marginal $(64,64)$, $T=1$ cell converges to
$1.46$--$1.55$. All cells are single runs at one seed, so individual
divergences carry the usual stochastic caveat; the mode-level separation,
however, is uniform across all six configurations.

% ============================================================
\section{Where the mass goes: $p_i$ profiles and total surrogate mass}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Hypothesis under test.}
A natural reading of the no-kappa failure is that the surrogate inflates the
soft mass inside the candidate pool: rather than \emph{redistributing} mass
across the boundary, the optimizer switches on features that are inactive in
the forward pass, so $\sum_i p_i$ grows. This section measures that directly.

\paragraph{The quantity.}
The LapSum soft assignment for a candidate is
\[
p_i = F\!\left(\frac{s_i - b}{T}\right),\qquad
F(z)=\begin{cases}\tfrac12 e^{z}, & z\le 0\\[2pt] 1-\tfrac12 e^{-z}, & z>0\end{cases}
\]
(the project's \code{laplace\_cdf}), and $\sum_i p_i$ over the top-$(K+J)$
pool is exactly the quantity LapSum's barrier solve drives to $K$. RBLapSum
does not solve for the barrier: it pins $b$ to the rank boundary,
$b=\max(b_0, s_{(K+1)})$, so here the mass is unconstrained and free to
drift. Note that $\sum_i p_i$ is a deterministic function of the score
geometry $(s, b, T)$ -- it is not an independent degree of freedom, but a
readout of that geometry in probability space.

\paragraph{How it was measured.}
The no-kappa runs died at steps 1620 and 1580, before their first checkpoint
at step 2000, so no saved states exist for them. The measurement therefore
re-runs each of the four runs from its own dumped \code{config.json} at the
same seed -- which reproduces the trajectory -- and measures every 50 steps
through \code{train()}'s \code{on\_step} hook, saving no checkpoints
(\code{analysis/probe\_mass.py}). The measurement is a forward pass under
\code{no\_grad} on one fixed batch, identical across all four runs, with the
gate buffers and RNG state it would perturb snapshotted and restored, so the
training trajectory is unaffected. Profiles below are block 4 at one token;
the mass was also recorded for all eight blocks at four token positions.

\begin{figure}[htb]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/mass_vs_step.png}
\caption{Total surrogate mass $\sum_i p_i$, normalized by $K$, against
training step (log scale); dashed curves are the part carried by the inactive
candidates, ranks $>K$. With the kappa correction (blue) the mass stays above
$K$ and the inactive part is preserved or grows. Without it (red) the
inactive part collapses -- to $0.8\%$ of $K$ at $(64,448)$ and $0.4\%$ at
$(128,128)$ -- and the total falls onto exactly $K+\tfrac12$.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htb]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/mass_profile_k64_j448.png}
\caption{$K=64$, $J=448$, $T=1$: $p_i$ against the feature's score at six

\paragraph{Result: the hypothesis is not supported, and the opposite occurs.}
Without the kappa correction the mass does not grow; it \emph{falls}, and the
inactive candidates are switched off rather than on:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llcccc}
\toprule
$(K,J)$ & mode & $\sum p_i/K$ & inactive$/K$ & $s_{(1)}$ & CE \\
 & & start $\to$ end & start $\to$ end & start $\to$ end & start $\to$ end \\
\midrule
$(64,448)$  & kappa    & $1.73 \to 2.10$ & $0.99 \to 1.31$  & $6.9 \to 12$   & $11.3 \to 1.71$ \\
$(64,448)$  & no kappa & $1.73 \to 1.01$ & $0.99 \to 0.008$ & $6.9 \to 4\times10^{7}$ & $11.3 \to 5.85$ \\
$(128,128)$ & kappa    & $1.06 \to 1.13$ & $0.37 \to 0.45$  & $12 \to 12$    & $11.3 \to 1.74$ \\
$(128,128)$ & no kappa & $1.06 \to 1.00$ & $0.37 \to 0.004$ & $12 \to 2.5\times10^{7}$ & $11.3 \to 7.90$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The end state of both no-kappa runs is $\sum_i p_i = K + \tfrac12$ exactly:
$p_i = 1$ for the $K$ active candidates, $p_i = \tfrac12$ for the boundary
feature itself, and $p_i \approx 0$ for all $J$ inactive ones. This is not
specific to the block and token plotted -- at the last measured step every
one of the eight blocks at all four token positions is within $1\%$ of
$(K+\tfrac12)/K$ in both no-kappa runs, while no block of either kappa run is.

\paragraph{Timing.}
The hardening precedes the loss failure rather than following it. At
$(64,448)$ the inactive mass has halved by step 450 and is below $2\%$ of $K$
by step 700, while the training loss only turns upward at step 750. At
$(128,128)$ the two coincide within the 50-step measurement interval (mass
collapse at step 900, loss turning at 850).

\paragraph{What the measurement does support.}
Because $\sum_i p_i$ is fixed by $(s,b,T)$, the collapse is the score-scale
inflation documented in \code{docs/scale-dynamics-note-neutral.tex}, read out
in probability space -- and it shows precisely how that inflation disables the
surrogate. Once $|s_i - b| \gg T$ for every candidate, $F$ saturates, the
kernel $\kappa_i = F'((s_i-b)/T)/T$ vanishes, and the boundary exchange
transmits no gradient at all: the run becomes a hard Top-$K$ whose surrogate
is numerically dead, which is consistent with its final loss ($5.85$ and
$7.90$) being far worse than an actual hard Top-$K$ baseline ($1.54$ at
$K=64$, $1.49$ at $K=128$). \textbf{(M)}

The kappa correction is what prevents this. It subtracts the
$\kappa$-weighted mean of the candidate gradients, $g = a - q\sum_i a_i$ with
$q_i = \kappa_i/\sum_j\kappa_j$, so $L_\kappa \mathbf 1 = 0$ and the common
mode -- a uniform push on all scores in the pool -- is removed by
construction. Without it the surrogate retains that component, and a common
push is exactly what inflates the score scale relative to the fixed kernel
width. This is the interpretation the measurement is consistent with, not
something the measurement isolates on its own. \textbf{(I)}
