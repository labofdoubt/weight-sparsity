\section{Setup}

Two-layer MLP on MNIST, $z = W_2\,\mathrm{ReLU}(W_1 x' + b_1) + b_2$ with
hidden width 1024, where $x' = \operatorname{AbsTopK}_K(e^{g} \odot x)$ keeps
the $K$ entries of largest magnitude of the rescaled input; $g \in
\mathbb{R}^{784}$ is trainable from $0$, pixels are in $[0, 1]$.  AdamW,
learning rate $10^{-3}$ with 100 warmup steps and a cosine decay to $10^{-4}$
over 5000 steps of batch 128, no weight decay, no gradient clipping, seed 1337
(the same data order and initialization for every run of a given $K$).

\textbf{Hard}: the backward passes the hard mask, so $g_n$ moves only through
samples whose support contains pixel $n$.  \textbf{DUST}: the same gate, with
every second optimizer step a DUST step (schedule $[1, 1]$): the gate input
$u = e^{g} \odot x$ of every sample is perturbed on all 784 pixels with
$\mathcal N(0, \sigma^2)$ noise, $\sigma = 0.1$, in $P$ draws (the
population), the tail is rerun, and
$$
\hat g_t = \frac{1}{P\,\sigma\,B} \sum_{i=1}^{P} \bigl(\ell^{(i)}_t - \bar\ell_t\bigr)\, a^{(i)}_t,
\qquad
\hat G_g = \sum_t \hat g_t \odot e^{g} \odot x_t
$$
is applied to $g$ by the shared optimizer; nothing else moves on a DUST step,
so a DUST run has 2500 hard steps for $W_1, W_2$ against 5000 for the hard
run.  $K \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$, $P \in \{128, 512, 2000, 8000, 32000\}$.
Training CE is the clean hard forward on both kinds of step; test CE and
accuracy are the deterministic hard forward on the 10\,000 test images.

\section{Results}

Figures~\ref{fig:k4} to \ref{fig:k32} and Tables~\ref{tab:k4} to
\ref{tab:k32} hold the measurements; all 24 runs finished.

\textbf{Small $K$.}  At $K = 4$ and $K = 8$ every DUST run beats the hard
backward by a wide margin: test accuracy 75.3--76.3\% against 67.1\% at
$K = 4$ (test CE 0.69--0.72 against 0.96), and 86.9--88.0\% against 84.6\% at
$K = 8$ (0.37--0.39 against 0.48).  The hard run's training CE is flat from
about step 1500 while the DUST runs keep descending.  Under the hard
backward $g_n$ moves only through samples that already select pixel $n$,
so the support is fixed early; the DUST steps give every pixel a gradient
and the support keeps changing.

\textbf{Large $K$.}  At $K = 16$ and $K = 32$ the test accuracies of all six
runs lie within 0.5 points of each other (93.3--93.9\% and 95.8--96.3\%).
The DUST runs reach a lower test CE (0.197--0.213 against 0.218 at $K = 16$;
0.123--0.132 against 0.140 at $K = 32$) with a higher training CE: at $K = 32$
the hard run's training CE is 0.022 against its test CE of 0.140, while the
DUST runs sit at 0.07 and 0.12.  Two effects are mixed here: the DUST runs
have 2500 hard steps for $W_1, W_2$ against 5000, and their support keeps
moving; this sweep does not separate them.

\textbf{Population.}  The gain is already present at $P = 128$ (two tail
forwards of batch 128 per DUST step) and grows little with $P$: from 128 to
32\,000 the test accuracy moves by about one point at $K = 4$ and $K = 8$ and
within noise at $K = 16$ and $K = 32$, and the training CE of the DUST runs
drops by 0.01--0.03.  At this problem size the estimator's variance is not
what limits the method; the cost of a DUST step is linear in $P$ (0.8 s at
$P = 32\,000$ against 12 ms at $P = 512$ on an RTX 3090).
